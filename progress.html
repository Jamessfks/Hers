Anna

An always-on AI companion who lives at the edge of your screen: a full-body VRM character in a transparent, always-on-top macOS window, driven by your own API keys. Electron and three.js; nothing hosted, nothing leaves the machine except the provider calls you configured.

v1.0.0 — shipped Persona, memory, voice, senses, attention policy, body, a settings window and a menu bar item, built and tested end to end. She now ships with a body of her own.

The 800ms budget

Past roughly a second, a reply stops reading as a person thinking and starts reading as a machine processing. Every structural decision in the codebase is downstream of this number. It only holds because the three stages overlap rather than queue.

0200ms400ms600ms800ms

  • 120ms Model time to first token
  • 180ms Enough tokens to make a first clause worth speaking
  • 90ms Cartesia time to first audio
  • 410ms Headroom, left for a bad network
  • 390ms Typical, against a budget of 800ms

The clock starts when the transcript is in hand, not at the end of your speech, and that is exactly what companion.test.ts asserts. Typing to her hits it. Speaking to her does not yet: the local VAD spends 420ms deciding you have stopped and transcription is a non-streaming round trip on top, so real end-of-speech to first audio is nearer 1.1–1.7s. Closing that needs streaming transcription with interim results. The number is quoted this way everywhere in the repo rather than quoted as end-to-end.

Fixed in 1.0

Real bugs, found by a critic pass against the quality bar and by writing the documentation. Each of these would have shipped silently.

  • She never made eye contact. Gaze aimed straight out from her own head, which for a figure framed head-to-toe is well above and behind the viewer.
  • Memory had a self-reinforcing loop. Retrieval refreshed a fact's recency, which guaranteed it was retrieved again — the first facts learned would have been pinned to the top of every recall for the life of the install.
  • Style examples masqueraded as history. A fresh install would have opened by asking how the interview went. There was no interview.
  • A dropped character did not survive a restart. The renderer can only make a blob: URL, and a blob URL dies with the window. Characters are now copied into place by the main process.
  • null could not clear a setting. So quiet hours could never be switched off. Now null clears a value and undefined leaves it alone, with a test for each.
  • The permission probe prompted on first launch. It asked for calendar access the moment settings opened, for a feature that is off by default — precisely the behaviour that teaches people to hit Don't Allow on reflex. It now probes only once that sense is on.
  • She stood in a T-pose, then with both arms in the air. The VRM humanoid rest pose is a T-pose, and the rest pose that brings the arms down was documented but never applied. Applying it from first principles got the sign backwards, which is how it was caught. Both directions are now pinned by a test.

Known gaps

Stated plainly, because a status page that implies otherwise wastes your time. This list is the changelog's own. The settings UI has left it; the rest is still true.

  • macOS only. Window behaviour and every sensor is AppKit-specific.
  • Speaking to her is slower than typing to her. The 800ms budget is measured from the transcript being in hand, and voice adds a 420ms VAD hang plus a non-streaming transcription round trip on top.
  • The video-avatar backends are a seam with nothing behind them. heygen and tavus are valid values of AvatarRendererId and no more than that.
  • She has no persistent mood. She remembers what you told her, but nothing about how you have treated her carries between turns.
  • No locomotion. sit_down holds a pose; she does not walk.
  • Unsigned build. macOS will need a right-click → Open on first launch.
